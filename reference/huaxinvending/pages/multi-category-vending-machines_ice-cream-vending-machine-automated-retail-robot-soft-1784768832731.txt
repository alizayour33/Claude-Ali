Product Description.

Click to download the product manual for the ice cream vending machine： download


A soft serve vending machine does the work of a soft serve counter in one floor-standing unit. The customer chooses a product on the touchscreen and pays; the machine drops a cup, makes the soft serve, adds the selected sauce or topping and delivers the cup to the pickup port. No one on staff has to make each serving. The operator refills, cleans and checks the back office on a schedule.

The Huaxin B86 and B86 Max are floor-standing soft serve ice cream vending machines from the B Series, with the same configuration apart from the front door. The B86 Max has a full-height window front door, so customers can see into the production area; the B86 has a closed front. The B83 Max, B84 and B85 run on the same platform and also differ only in front door and exterior. If you are comparing floor-standing and countertop options, see our overview of commercial ice cream vending machines.

What Makes the Machine “Robotic”?

The robotic effect comes from the way the equipment automatically coordinates several mechanical and digital systems.

After a customer places an order, the machine can complete a structured serving process:

1. The customer selects a product on the touchscreen.

2. The payment system confirms the transaction.

3. The cup dispensing system releases one cup.

4. Sensors confirm the position of the cup.

5. The lifting and positioning mechanism moves the cup into the production area.

6. The soft serve system dispenses the selected portion.

7. Optional sauces or dry toppings are added according to the configured menu.

8. The finished cup is moved to the pickup position.

9. The customer collects the product from the pickup port.

These automated movements create a robotic visual experience while also reducing manual serving steps.

The machine is not a toy robot and does not depend on a theatrical robot-arm performance. Its automated structure has a practical purpose: producing and delivering consistent soft serve products in a self-service retail environment.

Locations and Front-Door Options

In shopping mall walkways, attractions and cinemas, a vending machine competes with shop signs, displays and digital screens for customer attention. The B86 and B86 Max use a 32-inch touchscreen, an illuminated machine structure and full-body graphics to get customers to stop, and the whole purchase, from ordering and payment to pickup, happens at the machine.

Both models share the same ordering flow, serving process and capacity. For US and Canadian projects that require NSF/ANSI 6, choose the B86 Max in the 110 V / 60 Hz configuration (see Certifications below).

Automated Soft Serve Production

Once payment is confirmed, the machine dispenses the cup, makes the soft serve, adds the selected toppings and moves the cup to the pickup port. Under standard operating conditions, the B86 and B86 Max take approximately 15–20 seconds per serving. Actual hourly output depends on ordering intervals, payment speed, serving size, toppings and ambient temperature, so it is not a fixed figure.

The production speed helps the machine serve impulse purchases without creating the long manual process associated with traditional counter-operated soft serve equipment.

After start-up, the initial freezing time is approximately 15 minutes. The starting temperature of the mix and the room temperature affect this time.

The machine holds approximately 20 L of mix, has a 2 L freezing cylinder and stores approximately 160 cups. How many servings one mix refill produces depends on serving weight, overrun and recipe, so plan refill visits at busy locations around actual sales.

Soft Serve Menu and Mix
What You Can Put on the Menu

With compatible formulations that have been tested on the machine, the B86 and B86 Max can serve soft serve, frozen yogurt, sorbet and açaí-style frozen desserts. A new recipe goes through a machine test before it goes on the menu.

B Series machines run one base mix at a time. Menu variety comes from sauces and dry toppings: the same plain soft serve becomes several menu items with different sauces, chopped nuts or cereal crunch. The number of sauces and toppings is confirmed by configuration. Switching to another base, for example from soft serve to frozen yogurt, means changing the mix and using the matching recipe and machine settings.

To sell two bases at the same location, install two machines. One buyer in Canada ordered two B86 units, one set up for açaí and one for soft serve, each with its own exterior design. For açaí menus and machine graphics, see our açaí vending solution.

Toppings and Sauces

Dry toppings need a suitable particle size, such as nuts, cereals and dried fruit, and sauces need suitable flow properties so the machine can dispense them reliably. Test any new topping on the machine before launch.

Suitable for Unattended and Staff-Supported Locations

The machine is designed to complete the customer-facing sales process without an employee preparing every cup.

However, unattended retail does not mean that the machine requires no management. An operator still needs to refill ingredients and cups, inspect the machine, clean food-contact parts, review alerts, and arrange maintenance when necessary.

There are two common operating models.

Fully Self-Service Placement

The machine is positioned in a managed commercial location where customers order and pay independently. A local operator visits the machine according to sales volume and refill requirements.

This model is suitable for malls, tourist attractions, entertainment venues, and other locations with stable management and utility access.

Staff-Supported Automated Service

The machine is installed inside a shop, café, cinema, hotel, or branded retail area. Customers may still use the touchscreen independently, while nearby staff handle refilling and cleaning.

This model combines automated production with faster on-site support and may be suitable for locations with very high peak-time demand.

Remote management helps operators reduce unnecessary site visits and maintain better visibility across multiple machines.

Depending on the selected software configuration, operators can remotely review information such as:

• Order and sales records

This is valuable for distributors and multi-location operators because the robotic appearance attracts the customer, while the management system supports the business behind the machine.

A machine that looks impressive but cannot be monitored or maintained efficiently is difficult to scale. The purpose of the Huaxin platform is to combine front-end customer attraction with practical back-end operation.

Cleaning and Daily Maintenance

Routine maintenance is designed to be organized rather than complicated.

The machine has a one-touch cleaning program: after the operator adds clean water, it runs an automatic rinse cycle. The dispensing outlet, drip area and other food-contact areas still need manual cleaning and scheduled disassembly cleaning as described in the operating manual. Operators must also follow the cleaning schedule required for the selected mix, local food-safety rules, ambient conditions, and daily sales volume.

Typical operating work includes:

• Refilling soft serve mix

• Loading cups

• Refilling sauces or dry toppings

• Wiping the touchscreen and pickup area

• Cleaning removable food-contact components

• Running the configured cleaning procedure

• Checking seals, tubes, and dispensing parts

• Reviewing system alerts

• Keeping the surrounding ventilation area clear

A pasteurization (heat-treatment) option is available on compatible B Series configurations and is confirmed with the order. It does not replace routine cleaning.

Several functional components use modular or quick-removal structures to simplify inspection and replacement.

Site Requirements

Check these five points with the venue before you order.

Power

The standard electrical setup is 220 V / 50 Hz, with a 110 V / 60 Hz option, and the rated power is 1700 W. Voltage and frequency are set for the target market before the order is placed; the machine cannot switch on site. The socket must be properly earthed. Check it with the supplied phase tester before switching the machine on.

Ventilation

Leave about 50 cm between the back of the machine and the wall, and avoid direct sunlight or enclosed cabinets and alcoves. Hot weather raises the cooling load, so this clearance matters. The B Series is designed for indoor commercial locations; outdoor or semi-outdoor sites need a separate review of shelter, temperature, rain, sun, dust and electrical conditions.

Footprint and Access

The B86 measures 1950 (H) × 1080 × 940 mm and weighs approximately 362 kg; the B86 Max is 954 mm deep and weighs approximately 396 kg. Beyond the machine itself, allow space for customers to queue and collect their cups, and for opening the machine during refills. Check door widths, lifts and floor loading along the delivery route, and arrange local transport and unloading equipment, such as a forklift, before the machine ships.

Network

Remote management, sales data and low-supply or fault alerts all need a network connection: Wi-Fi, or a 4G or 5G module depending on configuration. If the mobile signal at the location is weak, tell us before ordering. Real-time remote functions are unavailable while the machine is offline.

After Delivery

Keep the machine upright and let it stand at its location for 24 hours before powering it on. Tilting and vibration in transit can push refrigerant into the capillary tube and evaporator, and starting the machine too soon can damage the refrigeration system. Never tilt the machine during transport or handling, and follow the start-up steps in the supplied user manual.

Certifications

The B86 and B86 Max are B Series models and hold the following certificates and test documents:

• CE: third-party CE certificates of compliance covering the Low Voltage (LVD), Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC) and Radio Equipment (RED) Directives

• RoHS: RoHS certificate

• IECEE CB: CB Test Certificate (IEC 60335 series) issued by TÜV Rheinland Japan

• NSF/ANSI 6: the B86 Max in the 110 V / 60 Hz configuration is certified by SGS North America for commercial indoor use; the B86 is not covered by this certificate

• Food-contact components: passed 38 third-party test items, including overall and specific migration

Huaxin operates an ISO 9001:2015 certified quality management system covering the R&D and production of commercial food equipment and vending machines, and holds an ETL certificate.

For US and Canadian projects, choose the B86 Max in the 110 V / 60 Hz configuration if NSF/ANSI 6 is required. Electrical safety certification requirements are reviewed with your account manager before ordering.

Certificate copies are available from your account manager. Requirements in other markets are confirmed for each destination.

FAQ
1. Can a soft serve vending machine run unattended?

The customer-facing process can be completed without a staff member preparing each cup. Customers select a product, pay, wait for automatic production, and collect the finished cup. An operator is still responsible for refilling ingredients and cups, cleaning the machine, checking alerts, and arranging maintenance.

2. Does the machine use a robot arm?

The B86 and B86 Max are not designed as theatrical robot-arm performance machines. Their robotic effect comes from the coordinated cup dispensing, sensing, lifting, positioning, soft serve production, topping, and delivery mechanisms. These structures support the actual vending process rather than serving only as decoration.

3. Can the machine keep up during busy periods?

Serving time is covered under Automated Soft Serve Production. At peak times, the queue depends on the whole transaction, including choosing, paying and collecting, not only on the seconds it takes to make the serving. Keep the menu short and fill up mix and cups before the rush. If one machine cannot keep up at a location, a second machine adds capacity and lets you sell a second base at the same time.

4. Soft serve vending machine vs traditional soft serve machine: what is the difference?

A traditional soft serve machine sits on a counter, and staff take the order, collect payment and pull each serving. A soft serve vending machine handles all three steps itself, which suits locations and opening hours where staffing a counter is hard to justify. Where staff are already on shift and serve hand-finished cones or sundaes, a traditional machine still makes sense. For a side-by-side look at cost, staffing and opening hours, read ice cream vending machine vs traditional soft serve machine.

5. How much does a soft serve vending machine cost?

The price depends on the model (B86 or B86 Max), the payment system, whether you need bill and coin modules, the pasteurization option, the topping system, interface languages, exterior branding and order quantity. Freight and trade terms are quoted separately. Send us your country, quantity and configuration needs for a formal quotation, and see our ice cream vending machine price guide for how the cost breaks down.

6. Can I see the machine working before I order?

Yes. We can share machine photos, videos or case materials based on your country and project type; anything involving a customer is shared with that customer’s permission. A site visit depends on whether a suitable local project exists and the operator agrees. Customer contact details, sales and profit data are never shared without authorization.

7. Can distributors order customized machines?

Yes. Huaxin supports distributor and project-based orders, including exterior branding, language settings, voltage adaptation, payment interfaces, training, spare-parts planning, and technical support. Commercial cooperation terms depend on the market, expected quantity, and project plan.
