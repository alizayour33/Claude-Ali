Importing an Ice Cream Vending Machine from China: Documents, Incoterms and Customs Checklist
Date：2026-10-26 Author：Huaxin

Planning to import an ice cream vending machine from China? Check the documents, who pays what under FOB, CIF and DDP, and the refrigerant and customs steps.

If you plan to import an ice cream vending machine from China for the first time, most of the risk sits in paperwork and handovers. The sea crossing itself is routine. In Huaxin's 2026 export orders, the delays and extra rounds of messages came from three places: names on the payment, invoice and bill of lading that did not match the company clearing customs; a trade term with no written scope, so nobody clearly owned the steps between the port and the door; and forwarders that would not accept a machine with refrigerant in its cooling circuit. All three can be settled before you pay the deposit.

This guide is for first-time importers and regional distributors. It covers the order sequence, the documents and what to check on each, who pays for what under the common trade terms, and the points that are specific to refrigerated machines shipped in wooden crates. Huaxin manufactures the machines discussed here, and the examples come from our own orders, with buyers anonymised. Freight amounts and landed-cost budgeting are in our guide to ice cream vending machine shipping cost and total import expenses. HS codes and duty rates are decided in your country, so this article explains what to give your customs broker instead of quoting a number.

Several later problems start at stage 1. Before production we ask each buyer to confirm the voltage and frequency, the interface language, the refrigerant type, and whether the stickers will use their artwork or ours. The refrigerant answer comes back at stage 5, because it decides how the forwarder treats the cargo (see section 4).

Stages 5 and 6 overlap. The carrier sets a cut-off for the shipping instructions behind the bill of lading, and the consignee details must be final by then. A buyer in Trinidad and Tobago was still checking with his customs office whether the machine could be cleared under a personal name on the day of that cut-off; missing it would have meant waiting for the next sailing. The export declaration and the final bill of lading are issued after the vessel sails, so your broker receives the complete set while the machine is at sea.

Customs systems generally want the price actually paid and the other charges itemised. US regulations, for example, require the invoice to state the purchase price and to list charges such as freight and insurance by name and amount [3]. If your broker asks for a revised invoice, find out exactly what is missing. We correct genuine errors and can show the machine, freight and insurance as separate lines, but the value always matches the sale.

Use one company name from payment to release

A buyer's name appears on five records: the payment slip, the signed contract, the commercial invoice, the bill of lading and the import entry. Our finance team requires the name on the payment to match the name signed on the contract. The invoice buyer and the consignee on the bill of lading should be the company that will clear customs. If those are different companies, tell us before the bill of lading is issued.

Three 2026 shipments show what happens when the names drift apart. A Canadian buyer paid two instalments from accounts in different names, so before shipping we confirmed with him that his broker could clear the machine under the first payer's company, and put that company on the documents. An Israeli buyer paid and signed under one business name and had the bill of lading made out to another company; after the machine was collected, its release was queried because of the mismatch. In Trinidad and Tobago, the broker asked for the bill of lading to be amended to show the company alone, without the buyer's personal name, because the licence for importing refrigeration equipment was being applied for in the company's name.

Agree how the original bill of lading travels

For the Trinidad and Tobago shipment, the port handlers would release the machine only against the original bill of lading, which had to be couriered to the buyer. At booking, ask your forwarder whether your port accepts a telex release or a sea waybill, or whether a paper original must reach you before the cargo does, and allow time for the courier.

Summary of Incoterms 2020 [1][2]. A contract can move any of these items to the other party, but only if it says so. DAP works like DDP except that the buyer handles import clearance and pays duty and taxes.

CIF stops at your port

Under CIF we pay the freight, but the transit risk is yours once the crate is on board in China, so damage at sea is claimed under the cargo insurance. Incoterms 2020 requires only minimum cover under CIF [1]. If you want broader cover, ask for it when you confirm the order. One buyer asked whether insurance was compulsory after his machine had already been handed to the forwarder, and by then the cover could not be changed. Destination port charges, customs clearance, duty and the delivery from the port also stay with you, so get a local estimate for them before you compare a CIF quote with a DDP quote.

What a DDP contract should say

DDP gives you one delivered price, and that price is only as useful as its written scope. Some countries require a local importer to handle import clearance, in which case DDP cannot be used and DAP is the usual alternative [2]. For ice cream vending machine DDP shipping, write the following into the contract, not only into the quotation or the invoice.

The named place: the full delivery address, not a city or a port.

The delivered price, with the same figure and the same scope in the contract as on the invoice. If the invoice includes door delivery and the draft contract does not mention it, correct the contract before you sign.

What the price includes: export clearance, sea freight, insurance, destination port charges, import clearance, duty, import VAT or GST, and transport to the address.

What it excludes unless stated: unloading from the truck, moving the machine inside the building, storage if you cannot receive on the agreed day, and permits you need as the operator.

The importer of record: which company appears on the import entry and whose tax number is used. This decides who can reclaim import VAT or GST and whose name is on the release documents.

What happens if customs asks for more documents or selects the shipment for inspection, and who pays for the inspection.

Which documents you receive afterwards, such as a copy of the import entry and the tax receipt.

We confirm DDP route by route, because it depends on what our logistics partners cover in the destination country. Where we cannot offer it, a CIF quote plus your own broker covers the same journey, with the costs split across two quotations.

4 Refrigerant, radio parts and crates
Refrigerant in the cooling circuit

Every machine leaves the factory with its refrigeration circuit charged and sealed, and the refrigeration system goes through a 48-hour pressure-hold test at the factory. Easy7 uses R290 and Power7 uses R404A. For B Series orders, the refrigerant is one of the items confirmed in writing before production.

The refrigerant affects how the cargo is classified. R290 is propane, a flammable refrigerant in ASHRAE safety group A3 [5]. Dangerous goods lists have a separate entry for refrigerating machines containing flammable, non-toxic liquefied gas, UN 3358 in Class 2.1, while machines with non-flammable refrigerants come under UN 2857 in Class 2.2 [6][7]. Exceptions depend on the quantity of gas and the transport mode; under US rules, for example, the exception for refrigerating machines holding a limited quantity of flammable gas does not apply to air transport [8]. In practice, the forwarder, or the consolidator on a shared-container booking, decides whether to accept the machine and on what terms.

Two buyers ran into this in 2026. An Israeli buyer booked his machine, charged with R290, as less-than-container-load cargo. The consolidator would not load it without a safety data sheet, because one hazardous item can hold up a container shared with other importers' goods. We supplied the hazardous-materials identification report we hold for the machine, and in the end the buyer moved the booking to a forwarder that accepted the cargo. In the Maldives, the buyer's forwarder would take the machine only with its refrigerant vented, which meant the buyer signing a release and arranging a technician to recharge it after arrival.

We do not recommend venting. Recharging at the destination opens a circuit that was sealed and pressure-tested at the factory, needs a technician qualified for that refrigerant under local rules, and adds a step before the machine can run. When you ask a forwarder for a quote, send the refrigerant type and quantity for your configuration together with the safety documents, and get a written answer on whether they accept the machine charged, for both shared-container and full-container bookings. If they will not, try a forwarder that ships refrigeration equipment regularly before you agree to venting.

Import controls can apply as well. Countries that have ratified the Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol must license the import and export of the hydrofluorocarbons listed in the Protocol's Annex F [9], and some countries extend licensing to equipment that contains controlled refrigerants. In Trinidad and Tobago, the buyer's broker had to apply for a licence before the vessel landed because the machine was classed as refrigeration equipment. Ask your broker whether this applies before you book.

Radio parts and card readers

Network-enabled machines and contactless card readers contain radio parts, and some countries approve those separately from customs. In Israel, the buyer's customs agent first had the machine classified, then asked for an application to the communications ministry covering the card reader, with an EU declaration of conformity, a data sheet showing the frequency range, a full test report and a power of attorney. Ask your broker early whether a telecom approval applies, and get those documents from the terminal supplier, which holds them for its own device. Choosing the terminal and provider is covered in our guide to ice cream vending machine payment systems.

Wooden crates, weight and the last mile

Each floor-standing machine ships upright in a wooden export crate, fixed inside and protected against moisture and shock. Wood packaging in international trade is regulated under ISPM 15, which excludes packaging made from wood processed so that it is free from pests, such as plywood [10]; solid wood packaging must be treated and marked. Ask us which applies to your crate and pallet, and check whether your country adds its own rules.

Give the trucking company the gross weight and crate dimensions from the packing list, not the machine's own weight. A B Series machine stands about 1.95 m tall and weighs several hundred kilograms before it is crated, and the crate adds to both. A Canadian buyer was still looking for a local carrier willing to move a crate of that weight in the week his machine was due, and when it arrived he had it held in the warehouse's long-term storage. Our handling instruction is to keep the crate upright from the container to the site. Plan for a forklift or a tail-lift truck, and measure doors and lifts along the route. After unpacking, our after-sales team asks for the machine to stand for 24 hours before it is switched on, then walks you through the first start, refilling and cleaning. Unloading equipment and delivery charges are covered in the shipping cost guide.

5 Ice cream vending machine customs clearance: HS code, duty and tax checks

Classification is decided under the tariff of the country you import into. The Harmonized System is standardised internationally to six digits, and countries add further digits for their own tariff lines [11]. Vending machines fall in heading 8476, which separates automatic beverage-vending machines with heating or refrigerating devices (8476.21) from other automatic goods-vending machines with heating or refrigerating devices (8476.81) [12]. Which subheading fits a machine that freezes liquid mix into soft serve and dispenses it into a cup is a decision for your broker and, if needed, your customs authority. The HS code on our export documents reflects the Chinese export declaration; treat it as a starting point for your broker, not as your import classification.

If the classification is uncertain or the order is large, ask whether your customs authority issues advance or binding rulings. In the EU, binding tariff information is a legal decision on a product's classification, binding on all EU customs administrations and generally valid for three years [13].

Duty and import VAT or GST depend on the classification, the customs value, the origin and any additional measures your country applies to goods from China, so we do not quote rates. Give your broker the specification sheet, photos, a short description of what the machine does (it freezes liquid mix into soft serve, dispenses it into a cup, keeps the mix refrigerated and takes payment), the commercial invoice and the trade term. The same set answers most of the questions customs raises later.
